## M1 — Understanding ESG in PPP construction (30 questions)

**Q1 [myth/fact]** True or false: ESG stands for Environmental, Social and Governance.
- [x] True
- [ ] False
- Explanation: E = Environmental, S = Social, G = Governance.

**Q2 [choice +photo]** Which ESG pillar covers ethics and anti-corruption?
- [x] Governance
- [ ] Environmental
- [ ] Social
- [ ] Financial
- Explanation: How the business is run — ethics, anti-corruption, oversight — is Governance.

**Q3 [choice]** Which of these is a SOCIAL impact?
- [x] Worker-camp conditions
- [ ] Dust emissions
- [ ] Soil erosion
- [ ] Concrete washout
- Explanation: Worker conditions affect people — a social impact.

**Q4 [choice]** The environmental footprint includes all of these EXCEPT:
- [x] Bribery
- [ ] Air
- [ ] Water
- [ ] Waste
- Explanation: Bribery is a governance issue, not an environmental footprint.

**Q5 [myth/fact]** True or false: a subcontractor's conduct on site is the contractor’s responsibility.
- [x] True
- [ ] False
- Explanation: Their conduct is flowed down to the contractor.

**Q6 [choice]** A 'stakeholder' is…
- [x] Anyone affected by, or who can affect, the project
- [ ] Only the shareholders
- [ ] Only the government
- [ ] Only the workers
- Explanation: Stakeholders include workers, communities, lenders, regulators and more.

**Q7 [choice +photo]** What is the FIRST step of the mitigation hierarchy?
- [x] Avoid
- [ ] Offset
- [ ] Restore
- [ ] Minimise
- Explanation: Always try to avoid the impact first.

**Q8 [choice]** 'Offset' in the mitigation hierarchy is…
- [x] The last resort for residual impacts
- [ ] The first thing to try
- [ ] A type of permit
- [ ] A KPI
- Explanation: You only offset what you couldn't avoid, minimise or restore.

**Q9 [multi]** Which of these are ENVIRONMENTAL impacts?
- [x] Dust
- [x] Effluent to a stream
- [ ] Unpaid wages
- [x] Clearing habitat
- Explanation: Unpaid wages is a social/labour impact.

**Q10 [choice]** Vulnerable groups need…
- [x] Extra care, as they are least able to cope
- [ ] No special attention
- [ ] To be excluded
- [ ] Higher wages than others
- Explanation: Impacts hit vulnerable groups hardest.

**Q11 [choice +photo]** The people who live around a site and feel its impacts are the…
- [x] Host community
- [ ] Lenders
- [ ] Regulators
- [ ] Shareholders
- Explanation: The host community lives with the project every day.

**Q12 [myth/fact]** True or false: good ESG can help the contractor win future work.
- [x] True
- [ ] False
- Explanation: Reputation and a clean ESG record win bids.

**Q13 [choice]** Noise and traffic disturbing neighbours is mainly a…
- [x] Social / community impact
- [ ] Governance issue
- [ ] Financial risk
- [ ] Cultural heritage issue
- Explanation: It affects the surrounding people — social.

**Q14 [choice +photo]** Whose job is ESG on site?
- [x] Everyone's
- [ ] Only the ESG team's
- [ ] Only management's
- [ ] Only the lender's
- Explanation: Every role owns a piece of ESG.

**Q15 [choice]** What does “ESG” stand for?
- [x] Environmental, Social and Governance
- [ ] Engineering, Safety and Growth
- [ ] Energy, Sustainability and Green
- [ ] Ethics, Standards and Guidance
- Explanation: ESG = Environmental, Social and Governance. On site, the Environmental and Social pillars are the ones you meet most.

**Q16 [choice +photo]** Which of these is an ENVIRONMENTAL impact on a construction site?
- [x] Dust from earthworks and haulage
- [ ] Unpaid overtime
- [ ] A land-compensation dispute
- [ ] Harassment at the worker camp
- Explanation: Dust affects air quality — an environmental impact. The others are social impacts.

**Q17 [order]** Put the mitigation hierarchy in the correct order — most preferred first.
- Answer: Avoid → Minimise → Restore → Offset
- Explanation: Always try to avoid the impact first; offsetting is the last resort.

**Q18 [choice]** Concrete washout, camp wastewater and sediment run-off mainly threaten…
- [x] Water
- [ ] Air
- [ ] Biodiversity only
- [ ] Cultural heritage
- Explanation: These are effluents that pollute water if they are not controlled.

**Q19 [choice]** What is the correct order of the mitigation hierarchy?
- [x] Avoid → Minimise → Restore → Offset
- [ ] Offset → Restore → Minimise → Avoid
- [ ] Minimise → Avoid → Offset → Restore
- [ ] Restore → Avoid → Offset → Minimise
- Explanation: Always try to avoid first; offsetting is the last resort.

**Q20 [choice]** “Vulnerable groups” are best described as…
- [x] People least able to cope with impacts, who need special attention
- [ ] The project's senior managers
- [ ] The lenders financing the project
- [ ] The largest subcontractors
- Explanation: Vulnerable groups need particular care because impacts hit them hardest.

**Q21 [multi]** Which of these are ENVIRONMENTAL impacts?
- [x] Dust from haulage
- [x] Concrete washout into a stream
- [ ] Unfair dismissal of a worker
- [x] Clearing vegetation
- Explanation: Dust, effluent and vegetation clearing are environmental. Unfair dismissal is a social impact.

**Q22 [fillblank]** Complete the definition of ESG.
- Answers: Environmental, Social, Governance
- Explanation: ESG = Environmental, Social and Governance.

**Q23 [hotspot +photo]** Tap the spot showing an ENVIRONMENTAL (air) impact on this site.
- Correct spot: Dust from earthworks
- Explanation: Dust from earthworks harms air quality — an environmental impact.

**Q24 [choice +photo]** SOLVE THE CASE: dust from your haul trucks is drifting over the homes along this road. Applying the mitigation hierarchy, what do you consider FIRST?
- [x] Whether the haul route can avoid the homes entirely
- [ ] Offering compensation to the residents
- [ ] Planting trees somewhere else as an offset
- [ ] Nothing — dust is normal on projects
- Explanation: AVOID comes first: can the route bypass the homes? Only then minimise (wet the road, slow the trucks), restore, and offset last.

**Q25 [choice +photo]** SOLVE THE CASE: community members walk this route daily — the same road your trucks now use. What is the right response?
- [x] Speed limits, flagmen and community engagement about safe timings
- [ ] Keep normal truck speeds — schedule is priority
- [ ] Ban residents from using the road
- [ ] Wait until an accident happens, then react
- Explanation: Community health & safety: control the hazard (speed, flagmen, timings) and engage the community — never shift the burden onto them.

**Q26 [order]** You notice a leaking fuel drum on site. Put your response in the right order.
- Answer: Spot — notice the leak → Report — raise it through site channels → Mitigate — contain it with the spill kit → Share — the lesson goes into the toolbox talk
- Explanation: Spot → Report → Mitigate → Share: every worker's ESG routine.

**Q27 [fillblank]** Complete the stakeholder rule.
- Answers: stakeholder, vulnerable
- Explanation: Stakeholders = anyone affected or able to affect the project; vulnerable groups are least able to cope.

**Q28 [categorize]** Sort each impact: Environmental or Social?
- Fuel spill into the soil → Environmental
- A land-compensation dispute → Social
- Generator exhaust fumes → Environmental
- In-migration pressure on the village → Social
- Explanation: Spills and fumes hit the environment; land disputes and in-migration hit people.

**Q29 [diagramtap]** The mitigation hierarchy. Tap the step where you COMPENSATE for what could not be prevented or repaired.
- Boxes: Avoid → Minimise → Restore → Offset · Correct: Offset
- Explanation: Offset = compensating for residual impacts — always the final step, never the first.

**Q30 [connect]** Link each ESG pillar to its example.
- Environmental → Concrete washout reaching a stream
- Social → Worker-camp living conditions
- Governance → Refusing a facilitation payment
- Explanation: E = the stream, S = the workers, G = the ethics.

## M2 — The regulatory framework (27 questions)

- [ ] True
- [x] False
- Explanation: Local law is the minimum (the floor); lenders require international standards on top.

**Q2 [choice]** PS8 concerns…
- [x] Cultural Heritage
- [ ] Labour
- [ ] Water use

- Explanation: PS8 — Cultural Heritage, incl. chance-find procedures.

- [x] Free, Prior and Informed Consent
- [ ] Final Project Inspection Certificate

**Q4 [choice +photo]** NESREA's role is to…
- [x] Set and enforce environmental standards
- [ ] Supply electricity
- [ ] Regulate banks
- [ ] Build roads

**Q5 [choice +photo]** The EIA Act requires…
- [x] An approved EIA before major works begin
- [ ] A tax return
- [ ] A marketing plan

**Q6 [choice]** Equator Principles Category A means…

**Q7 [choice]** Category C projects have…

**Q9 [myth/fact]** True or false: when local law and an international standard conflict, the contractor follows the cheaper option.
- [ ] True
- [x] False

**Q10 [choice]** The Equator Principles are used by…
- [x] Banks, to assess and monitor project finance
- [ ] Road contractors, to price jobs

**Q11 [choice]** PS3 is about…
- [x] Resource Efficiency & Pollution Prevention

**Q12 [choice]** Which Nigerian law requires an approved Environmental Impact Assessment before a major project proceeds?
- [x] The EIA Act

- [ ] The Finance Act

**Q13 [choice]** NESREA is Nigeria's…
- [x] Environmental standards & regulations enforcement agency

**Q14 [choice +photo]** The IFC Performance Standards are…
- [x] The international benchmark for managing ESG risk on financed projects

**Q15 [choice]** Under the Equator Principles, lenders categorise a project by…
- [x] Its level of ESG risk (A / B / C)

**Q16 [choice]** When Nigerian law and an international standard differ, the contractor applies…
- [x] The stricter, more protective requirement
- [ ] Whichever is cheaper
- [ ] Only local law
- [ ] Neither

**Q17 [choice]** Which is one of the eight IFC Performance Standards?
- [x] Labour & Working Conditions
- [ ] Corporate Tax Planning
- [ ] Advertising Standards
- [ ] Shareholder Dividends

**Q18 [order]** Order the assessment process on a project.
- Answer: Assess the impacts (ESIA) → Set the controls (ESMP) → Monitor performance → Report to the lender
- Explanation: Assess → control → monitor → report.

**Q19 [fillblank]** Complete the stricter-standard rule.
- Answers: stricter, international
- Explanation: Apply the stricter, more protective requirement — usually the international standard.

**Q20 [choice +photo]** SOLVE THE CASE: during excavation on this building site, workers uncover old pottery and carved stones. What applies, and what do you do?
- [x] PS8 chance-find: stop work at that spot, protect the find, notify the authorities
- [ ] Keep digging — the schedule is tight
- [ ] PS6 biodiversity: relocate the stones to a forest
- [ ] Sell the artifacts to fund community projects
- Explanation: PS8 Cultural Heritage: a chance-find procedure means stop at the spot, protect, and notify — works resume once cleared.

**Q21 [choice +photo]** SOLVE THE CASE: the road alignment must cross this green corridor. Under PS6, the project's biodiversity goal is…
- [x] No net loss — avoid, minimise, then restore or offset habitat
- [ ] Clear it quickly before anyone objects
- [ ] Pay a fine and proceed
- [ ] Biodiversity only matters in national parks
- Explanation: PS6 aims for no net loss of biodiversity — the mitigation hierarchy applied to habitats and species.

**Q22 [order]** Put the ESIA journey in the correct order.
- Answer: Screening — is a full assessment needed? → Baseline studies — measure before works → Impact assessment — predict the changes → Approval — the regulator signs off
- Explanation: Screen → baseline → assess → approve: no major works before the ESIA is approved.

**Q23 [fillblank]** Complete the Equator categories.
- Answers: A, C
- Explanation: A = significant/irreversible risk, B = limited and manageable, C = minimal.

**Q24 [hotspot +photo]** The ESIA baseline team surveys this area. Tap the PROJECT FOOTPRINT itself — the corridor the works will occupy.
- Correct spot: The road corridor
- Explanation: The corridor is the project footprint; the ESIA studies it AND the surrounding area it affects.

**Q25 [categorize]** Sort each rule: Nigerian law or international standard?
- EIA Act approval → Nigerian law
- IFC PS6 no-net-loss of biodiversity → International standard
- NESREA enforcement notice → Nigerian law
- Equator Category A scrutiny → International standard
- Explanation: The EIA Act and NESREA are the Nigerian floor; IFC PS and Equator are the international bar.

**Q26 [diagramtap]** The ESIA journey. Tap the step where you measure the environment BEFORE any works begin.
- Boxes: Screening → Baseline studies → Impact assessment → Approval · Correct: Baseline studies
- Explanation: Baseline studies capture the starting point — air, water, biodiversity, livelihoods — before works.

**Q27 [connect]** Link each body or rule to what it does.
- NESREA → Enforces Nigerian environmental standards
- EIA Act → Requires an approved study before major works
- IFC PS → The eight lender benchmark standards
- Equator Principles → How banks apply the standards to loans
- Explanation: NESREA enforces, the EIA Act requires the study, the IFC PS set the bar, Equator applies it to finance.

## M3 — ESG and the financing (30 questions)

**Q1 [myth/fact]** True or false: good ESG performance helps keep loan disbursements flowing.
- [x] True
- [ ] False
- Explanation: Meeting ESG conditions unlocks each disbursement.

**Q2 [choice +photo]** 'Conditions precedent' are ESG actions completed…
- [x] Before the first disbursement
- [ ] After the loan ends
- [ ] Only by the lender
- [ ] Never
- Explanation: They must be met before money is released.

**Q3 [myth/fact]** True or false: hiding an incident from the report is safer than reporting it.
- [ ] True
- [x] False
- Explanation: If the IESC finds it later, trust collapses — report honestly.

**Q4 [choice +photo]** Lenders build ESG into the loan because…
- [x] ESG risk is financial and reputational risk
- [ ] It is a legal tax
- [ ] It is charity
- [ ] There is no reason
- Explanation: Poor ESG threatens repayment and reputation.

**Q5 [choice]** Reputational damage from an incident mainly affects…
- [x] Future bids and the lender's ESG standing
- [ ] Nothing
- [ ] Only the weather
- [ ] Only payroll
- Explanation: A public incident costs future work and trust.

**Q6 [multi]** Which can result from a serious ESG failure?

**Q12 [choice]** An ESAP is…
- [x] An Environmental & Social Action Plan — corrective actions with deadlines
- [ ] A payment schedule
- [ ] A marketing plan
- [ ] A safety poster

**Q13 [choice]** The contractor mainly demonstrates ongoing ESG compliance to lenders through…
- [x] Self-monitoring reports, verified by the IESC
- [ ] Press releases
- [ ] Social-media posts
- [ ] Verbal updates

**Q14 [choice]** A serious, unresolved ESG failure can lead to…
- [x] Withheld disbursements, covenant breach or loan default
- [ ] A bonus for the team
- [ ] Automatic extra funding
- [ ] No consequences
- Explanation: Good ESG management is what keeps the financing flowing.

**Q15 [choice]** The contractor leaves an incident out of the self-monitoring report and the IESC later finds it. The likely result?
- [x] Lost credibility and withheld disbursements
- [ ] A reward for tidy paperwork
- [ ] Nothing at all
- [ ] A lower interest rate
- Explanation: Transparency is what keeps lender trust — hiding issues is far worse than reporting them.

**Q16 [order]** Order the ESG covenant cycle over the life of the loan.
- Answer: Appraisal → Conditions precedent → Disbursement → Ongoing monitoring
- Explanation: Assessed up front, conditions met, money released, then monitored throughout.

**Q17 [fillblank]** Complete the financing sentence.
- Answers: IESC, ESAP
- Explanation: The IESC monitors for the lenders; the ESAP is the corrective-action plan with deadlines.

**Q18 [choice]** An ESDD is…
- [x] The lenders' Environmental & Social Due Diligence before financing
- [ ] A daily site drill
- [ ] A discharge permit
- [ ] The contractor's marketing review

**Q19 [choice]** The gaps found during the ESDD typically become…
- [x] The ESAP — corrective actions with deadlines
- [ ] A press release
- [ ] A bonus plan
- [ ] Nothing — they are ignored

**Q20 [choice]** When does the ESDD happen?
- [x] Before the loan is committed
- [ ] Only after project completion
- [ ] Only when an accident occurs
- [ ] Never — lenders don't check

**Q21 [choice]** How is a lender-financed project audited over its life?

- [ ] One audit at the very end
- [ ] Only if the community complains

**Q22 [choice]** An IESC site visit finds a non-compliance. What normally happens to it?

- [ ] It is deleted from the report

**Q23 [choice +photo]** SOLVE THE CASE: this bridge is lender-financed and the first drawdown is next month. The grievance mechanism isn't running yet. What happens?

**Q24 [choice +photo]** SOLVE THE CASE: the IESC arrives tomorrow for a site visit. Two grievances are overdue and one corrective action slipped. What do you show them?
- [x] Everything — the full records, plus your recovery plan for the overdue items
- [ ] Only the good files; hide the overdue items
- [ ] Nothing — make them ask in writing
- [ ] A staged tour avoiding the problem areas
- Explanation: Transparency plus a recovery plan builds trust; anything hidden and later found destroys it.

**Q25 [order]** From audit finding to closure — put the steps in order.
- Answer: IESC finds a non-compliance → Corrective action added to the ESAP with a deadline → Action completed and evidence collected → IESC verifies and closes the finding
- Explanation: Finding → ESAP action → evidence → verified closure: that's the audit loop.

**Q26 [fillblank]** Complete the audit timeline.
- Answers: ESDD, IESC
- Explanation: ESDD before the money moves; IESC watching for as long as it flows.

**Q27 [categorize]** Sort each event: BEFORE financing or DURING the project?
- ESDD investigation → Before financing
- Equator category assigned → Before financing
- IESC site visits → During the project
- Self-monitoring reports → During the project
- Explanation: ESDD and the A/B/C category happen at appraisal; IESC visits and reports run for the life of the loan.

**Q28 [diagramtap]** The ESG covenant cycle. Tap the stage where the ESDD happens.
- Boxes: Appraisal → Conditions precedent → Disbursement → Ongoing monitoring · Correct: Appraisal
- Explanation: The ESDD is the lenders' investigation at appraisal — before anything is signed or paid.

**Q29 [diagramtap]** Same cycle. Tap the stage where money is actually released.
- Boxes: Appraisal → Conditions precedent → Disbursement → Ongoing monitoring · Correct: Disbursement
- Explanation: Disbursement is the release of funds — earned by meeting the conditions, kept by ongoing performance.

**Q30 [connect]** Link each financing term to its meaning.
- ESDD → The lenders' check before financing
- IESC → Independent monitor working for the banks
- ESAP → Corrective actions with owners and deadlines
- Covenant → A binding promise inside the loan
- Explanation: ESDD before, IESC during, ESAP to fix, covenants to hold it all together.
